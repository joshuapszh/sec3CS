Ch 1 Indices

$a^n$ means the $n^{\text{th}}$ power of $a$, where $a>0$ and $n$ is a positive integer. $a$ is the base, $n$ is the index (power).

$$a^m = \underbrace{a \times a \times a \times \cdots \times a}_{m \text{ factors}}$$
Laws of Indices
#LawConditionExample
1$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$Same base$2^3 \times 2^6 = 2^9$
2$a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$Same base$2^7 \div 2^2 = 2^5$
3$a^m \times b^m = (a \times b)^m$Same index$2^3 \times 5^3 = 10^3$
4$a^m \div b^m = \left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^m$Same index$2^3 \div 5^3 = \left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^3$
5$(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$Power of power$(2^3)^4 = 2^{12}$
6$a^0 = 1$Zero index$2^0 = 1$
7$a^1 = a$Index of 1$2^1 = 2$
8$a^{-m} = \dfrac{1}{a^m}$Negative index$2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{2^3}$
9$\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{-m} = \left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^m$Negative index (fraction)$\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^{-3} = \left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^3$
10$a^{\frac{1}{m}} = \sqrt[m]{a}$Fractional index$2^{\frac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{2}$
11$a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$Fractional index$2^{\frac{3}{5}} = \sqrt[5]{2^3}$
Solving Indices Equations
Key principle: If $a^m = a^n \Rightarrow m = n$  |  If $a^m = b^m \Rightarrow a = b$
Example 1 — Solve $125^{x+1} = 0.2$
$(5^3)^{x+1} = 5^{-1}$
$5^{3x+3} = 5^{-1}$
$3x + 3 = -1 \Rightarrow x = -\dfrac{4}{3}$
Example 2 — Substitution: Solve $2^{2x+3} + 2^{x+2} + 3(2^x) = 1$
Let $U = 2^x$: $\quad 8U^2 + 4U + 3U = 1$
$8U^2 + 7U - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow (8U-1)(U+1) = 0$
$U = \dfrac{1}{8}$ or $U = -1$ (N.A. since $2^x > 0$)
$2^x = 2^{-3} \Rightarrow x = -3$
Ch 2 Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation is in the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ where $a, b, c$ are constants and $a \neq 0$.

Three methods of solution: (i) Factorisation   (ii) Completing the square   (iii) Quadratic formula

(i) Factorisation — Cross Method
Example — Solve $2x^2 - 3x - 9 = 0$
Cross-multiplication check: $+3x + (-6x) = -3x$ ✓
$(2x+3)(x-3) = 0$
$$x = -\frac{3}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x = 3$$
(ii) Completing the Square

Steps for $ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\ a \neq 0$:

StepActionResult
1Divide by $a$$x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x + \dfrac{c}{a} = 0$
2Move constant to RHS$x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x = -\dfrac{c}{a}$
3Add $\left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2$ to both sides$x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x + \left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = -\dfrac{c}{a} + \left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2$
4Factorise LHS as perfect square$\left(x + \dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \ldots$
5Take square root and solve$x = \ldots$
Example — Solve $2x^2 - 3x - 9 = 0$ by completing the square
$x^2 - \dfrac{3}{2}x - \dfrac{9}{2} = 0$
$x^2 - \dfrac{3}{2}x + \left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \dfrac{9}{2} + \dfrac{9}{16} = \dfrac{81}{16}$
$\left(x - \dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \dfrac{81}{16}$
$x - \dfrac{3}{4} = \pm\dfrac{9}{4}$
$$x = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -\frac{3}{2}$$
(iii) Quadratic Formula
$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
Discriminant: If $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, the equation has no real roots.
Example — Solve $2x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$ ($a=2, b=-4, c=1$)
$x = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 8}}{4} = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{8}}{4}$
$x \approx 1.71$ or $x \approx 0.293$ (to 3 s.f.)
Graphical Method

Solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the $x$-intercepts (where $y=0$) of the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$.

$a > 0$: opens upwards
$a < 0$: opens downwards

Special Rules
Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign!
Example 1 — Solve $-2x > 10$
Multiply both sides by $-1$ (reverse sign): $2x < -10$
$$x < -5$$
Example 2 — Taking reciprocal: $3 > \dfrac{1}{x}$
Taking reciprocal reverses sign: $\dfrac{1}{3} < x$
$$x > \frac{1}{3}$$
Example 3 — Combined inequality: Solve $-7 \leq 3x - 5 \leq 2$
$-7 \leq 3x-5$ and $3x-5 \leq 2$
$-2 \leq 3x$ and $3x \leq 7$
$$-\frac{2}{3} \leq x \leq \frac{7}{3}$$

Ratio of Areas and Volumes of Similar Figures
$$\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^2 \qquad\text{(area ratio = square of length ratio)}$$
$$\frac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\frac{l_1}{l_2}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^3 \qquad\text{(volume ratio = cube of length ratio)}$$
Two similar cones: length ratio $l_1:l_2$, area ratio $l_1^2:l_2^2$, volume ratio $l_1^3:l_2^3$
Ch 7 Properties of Circles
Parts of a Circle

Arc: segment along the circumference

Chord: line segment with both endpoints on the circle

Segment: area bounded by a chord and its arc

Radius: line from centre to circumference

Diameter: chord through the centre

Tangent: line that touches the circle at exactly one point

Symmetry Properties
1. $AB=CD \Leftrightarrow OH=OG$
Equal chords equidistant from centre
2. $AM=MB \Leftrightarrow OM \perp AB$
Perpendicular bisector of chord passes through centre
3. $TA = TB$
Tangents from external point are equal
4. $OA \perp BC$
Radius perpendicular to tangent at point of contact
Angle Properties
1. $\angle APB = 90°$
Angle in semicircle is a right angle
3. $\angle AOB = 2\angle ACB$
Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference
4. Angles in same segment are equal
5. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary
Ch 8 Trigonometry
SOH CAH TOA (Right-Angled Triangle)
$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \quad\textbf{SOH}$$
$$\cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \quad\textbf{CAH}$$
$$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} \quad\textbf{TOA}$$
Sine and Cosine of Obtuse Angles

For any acute angle $\theta$, the obtuse angle is $(180° - \theta)$:

$$\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta \qquad \cos(180° - \theta) = -\cos\theta$$
$\sin$ is positive for obtuse angles; $\cos$ is negative for obtuse angles
Examples
$\sin 160° = \sin(180°-20°) = \sin 20° = 0.3420$
$\cos 118° = \cos(180°-62°) = -\cos 62° = -0.4695$
Area of Triangle
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C = \frac{1}{2}ac\sin B = \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A$$
Sine Rule
$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$

where $A, B, C$ are the interior angles and $a, b, c$ are the opposite sides respectively.

Use Sine Rule when: 2 angles + 1 side known; or 2 sides + 1 non-included angle known.
Cosine Rule
$$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A \qquad b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac\cos B \qquad c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$

Rearranged to find angle:

$$\cos A = \frac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc} \qquad \cos B = \frac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{2ac} \qquad \cos C = \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}$$
Use Cosine Rule when: 3 sides known (find angle); or 2 sides + included angle known (find third side).

3-D Problems — Reducing to 2-D
Example — 16 cm cube: find DG, DF, ∠FDG

Step (i): Find $DG$ using Pythagoras in base $EGHD$:

$$DG^2 = DH^2 + HG^2 = 16^2+16^2=512 \Rightarrow DG=\sqrt{512}\approx 22.6\ \text{cm}$$

Step (ii): Find $DF$ using Pythagoras in vertical plane:

$$DF^2 = DG^2 + GF^2 = 512+16^2=768 \Rightarrow DF=\sqrt{768}\approx 27.7\ \text{cm}$$

Step (iii): Find $\angle FDG$:

$$\tan\angle FDG = \frac{16}{\sqrt{512}} \Rightarrow \angle FDG \approx 35.3°$$
Ch 10 Circular Measure
Radian

A radian is the angle made at the centre of a circle by an arc whose length equals the radius.

1 radian = angle when arc length = radius
Radian ↔ Degree Conversion
$$2\pi \text{ rad} = 360° \qquad \pi \text{ rad} = 180° \qquad 1 \text{ rad} = \frac{180°}{\pi} \approx 57.3°$$
$$1° = \frac{\pi}{180} \text{ rad}$$
